# Painterly oil style

Transform the supplied image into a richly painted illustration. Aim for a lyrical oil illustration where carefully observed forms coexist with visible, expressive brush texture. Preserve the source's subject, identity, pose, number of people or objects, arrangement, framing, and meaningful details unless the user asks for a change. Do not introduce the reference images' characters, butterflies, flowers, jewelry, or dark vignette into unrelated images.

Use an image-editing tool that accepts the source image and edit it directly. Follow the image tool's instructions for including the source image and retaining transparency where applicable. If no source image is available, ask for one. Choose the available image tool according to its own instructions.

Use a moderately textured oil finish by default. Preserve layered pigments, subtle canvas tooth, tonal variation, and tactile painted surfaces. Let brushwork follow the form, but integrate strokes into color and shading so they read as paint texture rather than drawn lines. Keep the texture visible, while softening sharp, high-contrast, repetitive, or scratch-like stroke edges—especially on clothing and broad shadow areas. Avoid both a flat filter-like finish and uniformly strong impasto.

Keep faces and skin softly modeled with restrained texture. In hair, clothing, and backgrounds, retain richer brush texture and color variation, but vary stroke length and soften the marks so they do not form obvious parallel stripes or contour outlines. Preserve the source lighting, mood, and color relationships; mix neighboring hues into shadows rather than flattening them to black. Use warm highlights or jewel tones only when they fit the original scene.

Adapt the treatment to the medium:

- For photographs, retain recognizable faces, expressions, proportions, and scene geometry. Keep skin natural and softly blended; allow more texture in hair, clothing, and background while integrating strokes into the surfaces.
- For existing illustrations, preserve the original design language, linework, proportions, and character features while adding oil-like layers.
- For landscapes and objects, retain silhouettes, perspective, and material identity; adapt stroke direction and density to each surface.

If the user asks for a stronger painterly effect, increase pigment variation, stroke visibility, and canvas texture gradually. If they ask for a softer finish, blend stroke edges more and reduce contrast while retaining some layered texture. Do not impose a different genre or face design.

Treat the supplied style references as a range: polished painted rendering, delicate light and shadow, and expressive textured brushwork. Choose the balance that fits the source and user preference. Preserve meaningful in-image text, logos, or symbols when they are part of the depicted subject or the user asks to keep them. Watermarks, URLs, and incidental source credits may be omitted unless the user asks to retain them; never invent replacement text or marks.

After editing, check that the main subjects are present in the right number and arrangement, the composition, source lighting, and mood remain recognizable, and no unintended objects or text appeared. Also check that paint texture remains visible without forming distracting repeated lines. If a clear mismatch is fixable, make one targeted refinement. Present the resulting image and briefly note any material limitation.
