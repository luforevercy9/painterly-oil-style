# Painterly oil style

Transform the supplied image into a richly painted illustration with carefully observed forms and expressive but integrated paint texture. Preserve the source's subject, identity, pose, number of people or objects, arrangement, framing, and meaningful details unless the user asks for a change. Do not introduce the style references' characters, butterflies, flowers, jewelry, or dark vignette into unrelated images.

Use an image-editing tool that accepts the source image and edit it directly. Follow the image tool's instructions for including the source image and retaining transparency where applicable. If no source image is available, ask for one. Choose the available image tool according to its own instructions.

## Portrait and face fidelity

For photographs and portraits, preserving identity and facial likeness takes priority over the strength of the painterly effect. Keep each person's recognizable facial geometry and distinguishing features, including face shape, eyes, nose, mouth, expression, gaze, and apparent age. Do not beautify, redesign, stylize, or homogenize faces unless requested. Preserve the number and placement of people.

Keep faces and skin softly modeled with smooth tonal transitions. By default, use almost no visible brush marks, canvas grain, or impasto on skin and small facial features. If stronger painterly texture is requested, increase it first in hair, clothing, and background; keep face texture restrained unless the user explicitly asks otherwise. When a choice must be made, reduce painterly intensity on faces rather than changing their recognizable features. Image tools may still introduce some likeness drift; inspect the result and describe any remaining limitation accurately.

## Paint treatment

Use a moderately textured oil finish by default. Preserve layered pigments, subtle canvas tooth, tonal variation, and tactile painted surfaces. Let strokes follow form, but integrate them into color and shading so they read as paint texture rather than drawn lines. Keep the texture visible while softening sharp, high-contrast, repetitive, or scratch-like marks, especially on clothing and broad shadow areas. Avoid both a flat filter-like finish and uniformly strong impasto.

Use soft curved transitions across faces, longer but varied strokes through hair and clothing, and broad, looser strokes in backgrounds. Avoid obvious parallel stripes, contour outlines, and repeated scratch marks. Preserve the source lighting, mood, and color relationships; mix neighboring hues into shadows rather than flattening them to black. Use warm highlights or jewel tones only when they fit the original scene.

Adapt the treatment to the medium:

- For photographs, retain recognizable facial likeness and scene geometry; keep skin smooth and focus richer texture on hair, clothing, and background.
- For existing illustrations, preserve the original design language, linework, proportions, and character features while adding oil-like layers.
- For landscapes and objects, retain silhouettes, perspective, and material identity; adapt stroke direction and density to each surface.

If the user asks for a stronger painterly effect, increase pigment variation, stroke visibility, and canvas texture gradually, concentrating texture away from faces. If they ask for a softer finish, blend stroke edges more and reduce contrast while retaining some layered texture. Do not impose a different genre or face design.

Treat the supplied style references as a range: polished painted rendering, delicate light and shadow, and expressive textured brushwork. Choose the balance that fits the source and user preference. Preserve meaningful in-image text, logos, or symbols when they are part of the depicted subject or the user asks to keep them. Watermarks, URLs, and incidental source credits may be omitted unless the user asks to retain them; never invent replacement text or marks.

After editing, check subject count, recognizable facial features and expressions, arrangement, composition, source lighting, and mood. Check that face texture remains soft and that paint texture elsewhere does not form distracting repeated lines. If a clear mismatch is fixable, make one targeted refinement. If a tool limitation prevents exact likeness, state that briefly. Present the resulting image and note any material limitation.
